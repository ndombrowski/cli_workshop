**Description**: Research projects often generate data that is too big for a spreadsheet, too slow for a laptop, or spread over hundreds of files you don't want to handle one by one. The command line lets you automate these tasks, and a high-performance computer (HPC) lets you run analyses with more computing power. This hands-on workshop is aimed at researchers who have never typed a command before. We use DNA sequencing data as the running example, but the skills apply to any project with many files or large text-based data. Over two days, you will learn the following:

1. On day 1 you work on your own laptop. You learn how to use the command line and find your way around folders, download and unpack data, look inside large data files, and count and search through them with a few simple commands. You also learn how to repeat a step for many files at once and how to save your commands in a script.
2. On day 2 we move to Crunchomics, the UvA high-performance computer. You learn how to log in, copy your data there, install your own software and send analysis jobs to the cluster. As a worked example, we check and clean sequencing reads with SeqKit and fastp.

Requirements: before the workshop, please (1) request access to the Crunchomics HPC and (2) set up a terminal on your laptop and test your login. The attached instructions take about 30 to 60 minutes. Requesting access to the HPC can take a few days, so please start early. If something does not work, contact Nina Dombrowski (n.dombrowski@uva.nl) at the latest one week before the workshop.
